What it is

Stand on it. Play music.

At a concert, the bass doesn't just reach your ears — it shakes the ground you're standing on. It comes up through the floor, through your feet, your legs, your frame; half the show happens below your knees. The Bass Board was designed to reproduce that half — and only that half. It makes no sound on purpose. Your music plays at whatever volume you choose; the board delivers the floor.

Inside the platform: tactile drivers, an amplifier, and a DSP brain that compresses the bass band — squeezing a wide range of low frequencies into the range the drivers reproduce best. A kick drum lands like a kick drum. Not a buzz. Not a gimmick rumble. The note, in your body.

Every board is custom-tuned for a smooth, linear response from 10 Hz to 100 Hz — the whole tactile band, no peaks, no dead spots. And every board has an adjustable low pass, so you decide where the feel rolls off and the board keeps everything above it out of your feet.

Why it feels different

Tuned by an engineer whose ears paid for the rooms.

Most tactile gear just shakes. The Bass Board was tuned by ear by a man who has mixed live sound for 35 years. That's the point of it: feel what you'd otherwise have to turn up to hear. Club low end at apartment volume. Your monitors stay honest, your neighbors stay friendly, your ears last longer.

The board is built to be felt, never heard — if it ever makes a sound of its own, that's the drivers tapping the ends of their travel: a warning, not a feature. The DSP's limiter is set to hold you one notch under that edge — maximum feel, nothing strained, nothing rattling. That tuning is the invention. It's also the patent.
